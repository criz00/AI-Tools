AI Tools

These AI-built tools span nearly every stage of the talent management lifecycle, from workforce planning and talent acquisition to onboarding, learning and development, performance management, and career growth. Each pairs my talent management expertise with AI to solve a real business problem. Several were built for clients and are in use in their workflows today; others explore new ways to automate processes, support decisions, and speed up knowledge transfer.

They’re intentionally lightweight: no servers, no accounts, nothing to install, and free to explore. That makes them ideal for demonstrations, hands-on training, internal tools, and limited use. They aren’t built for enterprise-scale operations, sensitive data, or business-critical systems. Yet.

The same approach can scale into custom, enterprise-grade solutions with the security, infrastructure, and reliability your organization needs. Try a few below, and if you see one that fits a challenge you’re facing, let’s talk.

🔒 A note on your data: Nothing entered into these tools is stored, saved, or sent anywhere. Everything runs locally in your browser, and all information is lost as soon as you refresh or close the page.

Skills Taxonomy

A tool that helps companies track the skills they have and the skills they need.

SkillGraph reads documents a company already has, like job postings, project summaries, and course descriptions. It spots new skills, outdated skills, and skills whose meaning has changed, then suggests updates for people to approve. It also shows where the company is short on skills and gives hiring, learning, and AI tools one shared skills list.

This is a working prototype using sample data from a fictional company. It isn't connected to real company systems yet.

About Tool

SkillGraph keeps one shared list of skills for the whole company and helps keep it current. The prototype runs on sample data for a fictional company, Northwind Financial, with engineering, HR, and sales teams. All examples below come from that data.

What it does

  • Keeps the skills list current. Finds new skills, like “people analytics,” and flags outdated ones, like “cold calling.”
  • Finds skill gaps. Shows where the company is short on skilled people or depends on one person.
  • Tracks trends. Shows which skills are rising or falling in demand.
  • Matches people to jobs. Compares someone’s skills to a role’s requirements.
  • Recommends learning. Links skills to approved courses and articles, and shows where training is missing.
  • Shares one skills list. Gives hiring, learning, and AI tools the same skill names and definitions.

Who it’s for

  • The skills team runs it. Two to five people in HR, people analytics, or learning and development. They review SkillGraph’s suggestions each week and keep the skills list accurate.
  • Leaders and planners use it to make decisions. HR and business leaders, workforce planners, learning teams, and recruiters check skill gaps, trends, training, and role requirements.
  • Everyone else would use it through other tools. Employees, recruiters, and AI assistants would get skills information from SkillGraph through the company’s career site, hiring system, or learning platform. IT teams would set up these connections.

How it would work in a real company

  1. Read new documents. Each week, SkillGraph reads new job postings, project summaries, courses, and business plans.
  2. Suggest updates. It recommends changes, like adding “Revenue Operations” or merging “Recruiting” and “Talent Acquisition.” Small fixes happen automatically.
  3. Review and approve. The skills team approves or rejects the bigger suggestions. Each one shows why it was made and which documents support it. Every change is recorded.
  4. Spot skill gaps. Leaders see where they’re short. In the sample data, customer retention needs are only 60% covered, and only one employee has critical account management expertise.
  5. Recommend learning. SkillGraph links skills to approved learning. Every new resource needs approval, and each suggestion includes a link to check first. If approved, the learning resource can be added to the company’s LMS. In the sample data, about half of the skills the company needs have approved learning.
  6. Connect to other tools. Hiring systems, career sites, and AI assistants could use SkillGraph’s skills list. No outside tools are connected in the prototype yet.
  7. Save versions. Each quarter, the company saves a dated version of the skills list so teams can compare changes.

Performance Review Planner

Try it

Performance Review Conversation Planner

Prepare for your next performance review with a simple, step-by-step planner. Work through two short sections to prepare your employee and yourself, gathering performance data, examples, key messages, and plans for possible reactions. At the end, review and edit your plan, then download it as a Word document or PDF.

Try it

Talent Matrix: Prep

Prepare a draft 9-box placement before a talent review. Rate an individual employees’ performance and potential as Low, Moderate or High, and add evidence for each rating. Then drag the dot on the grid to adjust the placement. The tool flags missing, thin or generic evidence and prompts you to add a support plan for low ratings. When you’re done, it writes a talent summary you can bring to the review.

Try it

Talent Matrix: Calibration

Run a live talent review with your team on one shared 9-box grid. Add each person with their level and starting placement, then drag dots to recalibrate as the group discusses them. Dashed rings mark placements that are open to adjusting. A distribution view shows how the team is spread across the grid, and a change log records every move.

Try it
